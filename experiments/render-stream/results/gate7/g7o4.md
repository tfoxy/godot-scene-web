## Gate 7o4 result: GSW hd-patch step transactions on the g31

No design doc of its own: G7o3's "next lever" for GSW (the step transaction's p95 on the g31,
results/gate7/g7o3.md) with D32's rules.

**On the g31, GSW's hd-patch p95 moves from 10.61 to 9.99 ms against 0.5·T_p = 8.505–8.510 ms.
It is still a miss (`hd` is a stretch target on the g31, D25), and the 0.6 ms gain is about the size
of the phone's run-to-run band: main measured 9.33 ms in G7o3's run, and 10.33 and 10.61 ms in this
increment's two final benches.** Every round of both final benches is a miss except one at 8.505 ms,
exactly at the budget, in the build before the fix below. Scene-full, scene-patch and pace-patch
passed before and still pass. Byte identity holds: every desktop shot equals main's, and every golden passes natively
and as wasm.

The cuts are real natively: the step transactions run 11 % fewer instructions, and each frame
issues half the GL draw calls. They barely move on the phone. A phone profile and a diagnostic
(below) show why: **the g31's hd-patch p95 is set by the cold start of each frame, not by the
receiver's instruction count.** After about 12 ms idle per 16.7 ms frame, a frame starts on a slow
(clocked-down, cache-cold) core. A 4 ms busy-wait before each frame's timed work, used as a
diagnostic and not shipped, cut the same build's step-frame mean from about 9.6 to 6.9 ms and its
p95 to 6.8–7.3 ms. pace-patch costs 1.3 ms a frame on the phone for about 5 µs of native work.

Evidence (ignored): `artifacts/render-stream/gate7/g7o4/` in the G7o4 worktree. The final bench is
`final2-gsw/`, on the committed build. `final-gsw/` is the same bench on the build before the
`canvas-items` fix below, and is kept for its 10.33 → 9.88 ms; the phone profiles are `prof-base/`, `prof-ab/` and `prof-pace/`; the diagnostics are
`spin4/` (busy-wait), `calls-honest/` and `calls-drop/` (draw-call cost), `exp-white/`, `ab-l1/`,
`ab-l3/`, `ab-os/` and `ab-v1/` (levers and opt-level A/Bs), `shots-base-main/` and
`shots-final2/` (identity), and `msdf-*/` (the g7-4 bug and its fix).

### The levers taken

1. **Untextured draws merge with their textured neighbours** (`gsw-canvas-draw-list`
   `DRAW_UNTEXTURED` and `ITEM_UNTEXTURED`, the `canvas-items` executor, `rs-core`'s assembly).
   An item whose draws are all white (and not MSDF) carries `ITEM_UNTEXTURED` in its row, and its
   builtin-program draws carry `DRAW_UNTEXTURED`. Such a draw's texture and sampler no longer end a
   run (`mergeable` and the new `merged` take the run's state). The call binds the run's first
   textured draw's texture, and the builtin fragment stage uses `texel = 1.0` for that item. That
   is exactly the white texel's value, so pixels are unchanged.
   - An all-untextured call still binds the white texel. The first version kept whatever was
     bound, since the fragments ignore it. Validation's `g7-4` MSDF legs then lost every draw of
     the frame after the MSDF atlas was re-uploaded (seq 11 of gate 4e's `msdf` capture): the
     executor's binding cache is not the whole GL state. The pixel suite and the bench inputs had
     not caught that. Binding white keeps the merges and costs at most one bind per such call.
   - hd-patch drops from 387 to 190 calls per frame, and scene from 43 to 22.
   - Phone draw p50 falls from 1.59 to 1.44 ms (`ab-l1/`).
   - Halving the calls by sabotage (`drop-draws`) costs 0.31 ms (`calls-drop/`), so a call costs
     about 1.6 µs on the phone.
2. **`IdTable` replaces the resolved state's six `BTreeMap<Id, _>`** (`rs-core/src/idtable.rs`).
   A slot array covers ids below 65536 and indexes a value arena, with a `BTreeMap` fallback
   above that. Iteration is still ascending id, so it is a drop-in. A unit test checks it against
   `BTreeMap` under 20 000 mixed operations. Natively, decode-only instructions on hd-patch fall
   12 % (134.3 M → 118.1 M).
3. Smaller cuts:
   - The payload tables and the program table hash a digest with the id hasher, not SipHash.
     Nothing iterates them in an order-dependent way: each sorts or keeps its own touch order.
   - The named materials and shaders are computed once per transaction, not five or six times.
   - `expected_calls` is recomputed only after an assembly.
   - A polygon reserves its vertices and indices once.

Native step transactions (hd-patch seq 11…111; accept, prepare, needed and apply; payload
provide excluded) over 20 replays, counted with `perf stat` on a step-only harness: **485.9 M →
433.3 M instructions (−10.8 %)**. Wall time is not reported here: the machine sat at load 6–9
throughout (other sessions plus this increment's own validation), and the wall-time minima moved
±10 % between identical runs.

### Where the g31's hd-patch p95 goes

This is a phone profile, `prof-ab/`: the core's phase timers are on (`--profile`), three
interleaved rounds, and the means are over the 36 step transactions per arm (seq 11 … 121, three rounds) (µs). Timers inflate
the totals somewhat; the split is what counts.

| Phase | main | this tree |
| --- | --- | --- |
| draw (`execute`: GL calls, uploads, blit) | 1939 | 1833 |
| lower | 1075 | 1153 |
| resolve (validator apply + reindex) | 982 | 947 |
| assemble | 799 | 830 |
| walk (cull) | 755 | 791 |
| classify | 768 | 763 |
| meta (record decode) | 615 | 674 |
| post_checks | 420 | 393 |
| dependency, materials, mesh_sync, prepare, needed, pre_checks, bindings, texture_sync | 1306 | 1293 |
| **step total** (decode + apply + draw + provide + programs) | 9616 | 9661 |
| non-step total | 5054 | 4746 |

**What no single lever removes:**

- **No phase dominates.** Eight phases each take 0.4–1.2 ms of a 9.6 ms step frame. Natively the
  same phases take 30–90 µs each, so the phone runs them 12–25× slower. Its own fast compute ratio
  (G7x: wasm about 11× desktop) does not explain that.
- **The phone does not track instruction count.** The step transaction's native instructions fell
  11 % (and its native time 20–30 % in the early, quieter measurements). Its phone phases did not
  move (resolve 982 → 947, walk 755 → 791, assemble 799 → 830). Phone time per phase is not
  proportional to the work.
- **Each frame pays a fixed "cold start".** pace-patch is one transform patch of one item, about
  5 µs natively. On the phone it costs decode 0.47–0.64 ms, apply 0.15–0.26, draw 0.49 and
  programs 0.09: 1.3–1.6 ms per frame (`prof-pace/`). post_checks alone is 145 µs, against 0.5 µs
  natively.
- **A warm core makes the same work about 30 % faster.** `spin4/` (diagnostic only) busy-waits
  4 ms on the main thread before each frame's timed work. Same build, same input:

  | | step mean | p50 | p95 per round |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | with the busy-wait | 6.9 ms (decode 1.74, apply 3.10, draw 1.62) | 3.6 ms | 6.8–7.3 ms |
  | without it | about 9.6 ms | 5.0 ms | 9.0–10.1 ms |

  One unprofiled round in `exp-white/` also ran everything at half its usual time (p50 2.6 ms),
  with no change in the recorded cpufreq caps.
- **The cause is the governor and idle states, not the receiver.** `schedutil` (A55 500–1800 MHz,
  A75 850–2000 MHz, `time_in_state` readable) sizes the frequency to a utilisation of about 30 %.
  After 12 ms idle a frame starts clocked down and cache-cold. G7o2 saw the same effect from the
  other side: cutting steady-state work slowed the step frames.

**What would move it, and was not done here:**

- **Pre-warming with work that must happen anyway.** Run D31's deferred record digest (1.4 ms on
  hd-patch, now right after the frame) just before the next frame instead. That is still off the
  frame path, and the total work is unchanged. It is a scheduling choice that deliberately steers
  DVFS, so it is the user's call; it is not taken here.
- **A worker for decode and validation** (receiver-decode-profile.md, tier 1 item 4): a second
  thread with its own clock behaviour.
- **Live-mode ordering.** The bench decodes inside the rAF callback (file mode, one record per
  frame). The live receiver decodes in `onmessage` ahead of the rAF, which wakes the core earlier.
  The D32 bench may therefore be more pessimistic than live hd.

### Experiments that did not hold

- **opt-level `s`.** One A/B (`ab-os/`, three rounds) put hd-patch at 8.03 ms p95 against
  O3's 9.55. It made pace-patch and scene-patch 30 % slower and doubled `evidence_hash`. A second
  A/B with `s` everywhere but `sha2` (`ab-v1/`) was worse than O3 on every input (hd 11.53 vs
  10.37). The first result was phone variance. O3 stays. Per-package opt-levels do not survive fat
  LTO (that build came out larger than O3).
- **`simd128`.** It gave no measurable change in Node.

### Deviations

- **Downstream consumer check, by construction only.** The scratch archive copy of
  `../sts2-couch-coop` could not typecheck: its `frontend/node_modules` has no `vue-tsc`, `vitest`
  or `vite` binaries, and installing them needs the network. The consumer resolves
  `packages/*/src` TypeScript and `build-web.sh`'s default `rust-prototype` build. This increment
  changes no TypeScript under `packages/canvas`, and that build is byte-identical to `main`'s. The
  new draw-list flags are only inside the `canvas-items` feature, which `build-web.sh` does not
  enable (D30).
- **The phone runs were not at host load below 4.** The machine stayed at 5.5–9.3 the whole
  session. The A/Bs are interleaved, and the result is labelled with the load.
