# W11-D: render-stream/11, the wire that makes long sessions viable (2026-10-25)

**/11 bounds a viewer's memory by the resolved state and cuts STS2 combat's wire by 17 % and its
WebSocket messages from 103 to about 1 per transaction.** Replaying the kept STS2 recordings
through rs-core's validator gives these predictions:

- **/10.** A GSW viewer's payload store gains 102 entries per combat transaction, for good. At
  P3-SP's 49.6 applies a second that is 6.6 MB of wasm heap a second. P3-SP measured 6.7.
- **/11.** The store never holds more than 876 entries (`a8-2`) or 296 (`late-1`). Those are
  the payloads the state names, plus what the transactions in flight bring.

The design is [protocol/render-stream-11.md](../../protocol/render-stream-11.md). This document
records its measurements: the predicted wire bytes and receiver memory under /11 against /10, and
the forensics of P3-SP's dropped GSW store bound.

/11 is /10 plus:

- **P3-SP's prune rule**: after every transaction both ends keep C ∩ names(S).
- **P3-LV's dynamic mesh surfaces**: a mesh that animates sends its bytes by mesh id, inside the
  transaction.
- **P3-PM's multimesh tier** (PM1).

Two consequences carry most of the design:

- **The two ends cannot disagree.** Every receiver accepts every record. Folding, resync and
  superseded transactions skip applies, not accepts. So the host and receiver carried sets are
  equal after every record.
- **The host needs no carried set.** After a transaction S the set is inline(S). So a
  connection's records are a function of its base and target, which P3-SP's shared encodes can
  share whole.

## Method

- **Recordings.** C3's `a8-2` (mod init: menu, map, combat; 4,960 transactions over 5,000 frames)
  and `late-1` (armed at the map, then combat; 1,784 transactions). Both are `/8` patch recordings
  with directory delivery, kept in
  `artifacts/render-stream/p3/fidelity-data/{a8-2,late-1}/capture/`. The store's `index.jsonl`
  gives every payload's size, and each GRM1 file's header its meta length.
- **Tool.** A scratch Rust binary over `rs-core`'s `Validator`
  (`artifacts/render-stream/p3/w11-d/tool/`). It accepts every record and, after each
  transaction, reads the resolved state's names: `ok` image hashes, `ok` mesh surfaces, and shaders
  whose source is a payload. It then simulates one live viewer that takes every transaction,
  under three regimes:
  - **/10**: render-stream/2's lifelong carried set. A hash goes inline at its first naming if its
    payload is at most `GRC_RESOURCE_INLINE_MAX_BYTES=4096` (the hand-off's live setting), or it
    is a GET.
  - **/11, prune only** (`GRC_MESH_DYNAMIC=off`): the records before T are
    inline(T) \ inline(B). A hash that returns is counted as a re-send.
  - **/11** (`auto`, K = 4): as above, but a mesh becomes dynamic at its second change within 4
    frames and stays dynamic. A dynamic mesh's changed bodies (GRM1 without its 12-byte header and
    meta) go into `mesh_u8`. Each sent mesh entry gains `,"u8":…` (+10 B), and a dynamic surface's
    `"hash":"<64 hex>"` becomes `"hash":null` (−62 B). Every transaction gains the two new blocks'
    framing (+44 B).
- **What counts.**
  - Bytes per transaction: the transaction record plus every resource record. A record's size is
    the exact framing of its meta, plus a WebSocket frame header.
  - Messages: one per record.
  - The receiver's store: /10 keeps every hash ever named. /11 keeps names(S) ∪ names(S−1): the
    newest state and the one being drawn.
- **Scenes** by frame:
  - `a8-2`: menu 16–1193, map 1194–1684, combat 1686–5000;
  - `late-1`: map 1182–1743, combat 1745–2973.

  The first (full) transaction is reported separately as the join.
- **Heap.** The per-entry heap cost of a GSW store entry is fitted to P3-SP's measured combat slope
  (6.7 MB/s at 49.6 applies/s): 1.3 KB per held entry. So the /10 heap row restates the
  measurement rather than predicting it. P3-SP's dropped-bound comment measured 2.7 KB an entry on
  another leg. The /11 rows do not depend on this figure: their entry counts are flat.
- **Upper bound on churn.** The recordings hold every host frame. A live viewer at window 2 folds
  some frames, which skips their poses, so it receives fewer records per second than this model.
  The per-transaction figures are what one sent transaction costs.

- **The bytes.** /11 sends the same mesh content as /10, but as 39 KB of bodies rather than 76 KB
  of records. The 37 KB difference is:
  - 102 record headers and WebSocket frames;
  - GRM1's 12-byte header and meta in each payload (186 B on average);
  - the 64-hex hash each re-sent mesh entry names.
- **The messages.** P3-LV found that the GDScript receivers spend their main thread on per-record
  messages. Their combat input goes from about 103 messages a transaction to 1.
- **Prune alone in combat costs almost nothing:** 0.19 re-sends a transaction, 136 B. Combat
  content does not recur, and only the poses churn.
- **At 49.6 transactions a second** (P3-SP's one-viewer rate): /10 is 12.5 MB/s and /11 10.4 MB/s
  for `a8-2`, and 10.3 and 8.2 for `late-1`.

- **The menu is where prune alone costs.** Its 21 animated meshes loop, so their poses recur after
  leaving the state. Prune-only re-sends about 6 records a transaction, and the bytes go up 6 %.
  With `auto` those meshes are dynamic, the re-sends go to zero, and /11 is 8.8 % below /10.
- **The map does not animate meshes.** /11 adds only the new keys and block framing (+44 B).
- **Out of band.** Prune lets a receiver drop out-of-band textures that leave the state. Over the
  whole menu that costs 4 GETs, which the browser's HTTP cache would usually answer, and none in
  combat.

A late joiner's first (full) transaction, at the last combat transaction:

| - | -: | -: | -: |
| `a8-2` | 281 (300,316) | 175 (221,724) | 39,880 |
| `late-1` | 131 (101,532) | 25 (22,940) | 39,880 |

The out-of-band textures are the same under both versions, and they dominate a combat join:
P3-LV's 1.7–3.3 s first apply stays bound by them, not by /11.

## Receiver and host memory over 20 minutes of combat

At 49.6 applies a second (P3-SP's one-viewer `p3sp-new-3`), `a8-2`'s rates give:

| | /10, 20 min | /11 |
| - | -: | -: |
| GSW payload store entries | +6.1 million | ≤ 876 (≤ \|names\| + in-flight) |
| GSW wasm heap | +6.6 MB/s: wasm32's 4 GB about ten minutes into combat (P3-SP) | flat; the state's textures plus 40 KB of dynamic geometry |
| rs-core validator carried map (~80 B a hash) | +487 MB | ≤ 281 inline names plus the records in flight |
| host carried index (P3-SP, ~40 B a hash, all viewers) | +244 MB | none (records from base and target) |

What does not shrink is the state itself. In `a8-2`, combat names 955 MB of texture payloads, and
the receiver holds those under both versions. That is the T1 bound ("one state plus a constant"),
and `/9`'s Basis delivery is the lever on it, not `/11`.

## Multimesh

The recordings carry no multimesh buffers: `multimesh_set_buffer` was not hooked. P3-PM's
prediction stands for PM1: 2,944 B per combat frame inline in `mm_f32`, which is +1.4 % on /11's
210 KB, and 64 B on the menu.

## The P3-SP re-fetch storm, from its counters

P3-SP's dropped bound is preserved in its snapshot branches (`2aebd324`, dropped in `92bddd81`).
It evicted the oldest-touched unreferenced payloads beyond 65,536 entries or 256 MiB, in live
mode with HTTP fetch. Its two fixture legs' receiver counters
(`artifacts/render-stream/p3/p3-sp/fixture/*/rx-perf.json`):

| leg | floor | GETs | bytes | GETs beyond the 187 textures | their mean size |
| --- | ----- | ---: | ----: | ---------------------------: | --------------: |
| `long-rx-before` (no bound) | — | 187 | 245,126,332 | 0 | — |
| `long-host-after` (20 min) | resident set | 63,409 | 295,441,414 | 63,222 | 796 B |
| `long6-rx-after2` (6 min) | resident ∪ names(newest accepted state) | 16,553 | 258,104,570 | 16,366 | 793 B |

- Every leg fetches the fixture's 187 textures once, at 1,310,836 B each (`long-rx-before`).
- What the bound added averages 793–796 B a GET. That is one fixture mesh surface: GRM1 with 24
  vertices. So the storm re-fetched **inline-carried mesh poses**. It did not re-fetch textures,
  and shaders were at most a small share.
- Every fixture pose is new: its heights carry `frame * 0.001`. And the second floor protected every
  hash the newest accepted state names. So each re-fetched pose was evicted **after its resource
  record was accepted and before the transaction naming it was**. GSW accepts in `onmessage` and
  applies (and so evicts) in rAF, which can run between a transaction's records and the
  transaction.
- /11's retention set H = C ∪ names(S_n) ∪ names(S_d) contains exactly those records: they are in
  C until their transaction prunes. And at /11 a GSW viewer never GETs an inline-sized hash
  (`inline-fetch-zero`).
- Not shown by the counters: why the LRU order reached those newest entries. It no longer matters
  under /11, because its retention is exact and recency plays no part.

## Validation

This is a design increment: docs only, no code.

- `validate-increment.sh --plan` maps the diff (three protocol docs and this file) to no runs.
- The measurements are reproducible from `artifacts/render-stream/p3/w11-d/tool/`:
  ```
  cargo build --release
  w11d-sim <recording> <store> 4096 4 > out.jsonl
  python3 w11d-agg2.py
  ```
- The validator accepted all 4,960 and 1,784 transactions without an error. That replays C3's
  recordings through `main`'s rs-core once more.

## Deviations

- **Prediction, not prototype.** No codec was built, as the brief asked. The /11 numbers are
  computed from the recordings' resolved states, with the transaction meta change counted per key
  rather than encoded. W11a's goldens and W11b's capture will measure the real bytes, and W11b's
  `capture-scale` legs the real host cost.
- **Every transaction, one viewer.** A folding viewer receives fewer poses per second than the
  recording holds. That makes the /10 growth rate an upper bound per frame, and P3-SP's measured
  6.7 MB/s confirms it at 49.6 applies a second.
- **Heap per entry fitted, not measured** (Method). The /11 bound is an entry count from the
  recordings and does not depend on it.
- **Storm cause narrowed, not closed** (above). The design makes it impossible, which the brief
  accepts as an alternative.
- **`late-1` arms at the map**, so its menu row is the single transition transaction and is not
  reported.
