# W13f: render-stream/13 live formation cost (2026-10-30)

W13f is the fix W13z named ([w13z.md](w13z.md) "What fails, and the fix"): /13 formation on HC5's
encode thread no worse than /12's in STS2, and no /13 history released on the main thread. It is
capture-only; the wire is byte-identical. The W13f row of [render-stream-13.md](../../protocol/render-stream-13.md)
"Increments" and its "As built (W13f)" section say what was built.

## Verdict

**Accepted (2026-10-30).** The orchestrator accepted the formation residual below: W13z's bar
guards the main thread, which now matches /12, and the ~0.07 ms a frame sits on HC5's encode
thread. The couch-coop switch to /13 landed as couch `7124aa83`.

**The main-thread half passes; the formation half does not.** The /13 history is no longer released
on the main thread (0 samples there in every /13 window, against 0.04–0.07 ms a combat frame at
W13z), and the live predictor now reuses its history (3 states allocated per connection over the
whole leg, against one a transaction). Formation fell from **+31 %** over /12 in combat (W13z, two
pairs) to **+12 %** (four pairs over two launches: +13, +28, −10 and +15 %), about **+0.25 M
cycles (≈ 0.07 ms) a combat frame** on the encode thread. The menu stays +13–48 % (one /12 window
per launch) and the map +11 % on average, inside the windows' spread.

**The residual.** The predictor costs about 0.55–0.65 M cycles a combat frame in the game whatever
its instruction count. W13f cut its instructions by 56 %, and that halves it in a warm cache, but in
the game it runs cold: its cost there is fetching H1 and H2 from memory. zstd saves 0.15–0.3 M cycles
on /13's message, which is as large as /12's before coding. So the predictor's remaining work is
larger than /13's zstd saving, by about 0.25 M cycles a combat frame. The orchestrator decides
(the main thread is what HCz's targets guard; this cost is on HC5's encode thread). The couch-coop
switch waited for that decision (accepted, above).

## What changed (`capture/src/rs13_predict.{h,cpp}`, `rs_live.{h,cpp}`)

1. **History reuse beside the memo.** W13p refilled the state leaving the history (H2) only when the
   predictor was its sole holder. Live, the hub keeps `before` (H1, H2) for the memo key, so H2
   never was, and every transaction allocated a state and its bodies. Now the H2 that cannot be
   reused stays as the predictor's *spare*, and the next transaction refills the spare (R(T−3) by
   then), which the memo and the lockstep connections have let go of. The file lanes still reuse H2
   directly. `Predictor::reuse_stats()` counts the states reused and made, and live-summary's
   `predict` object gains `states_reused` and `states_made`.
2. **Release on the encode thread.** The batch-end job resets the memo's /13 codings (`before`,
   `after`, the /13 bytes) and the typed metas before it hands the memo to the main thread. The
   spare also holds each dropped state's last reference, so states die on the encode thread at the
   next transaction. `rs13::states_released_here()` counts the states destroyed per thread, for
   the test.
3. **Coding.** `code_words<Order>`: the residual and the byte-plane transpose take 16 and then 4
   words a step on SSE2, with plain words for the rest. The writer codes each surface stream by
   stream (`code_surface_fwd`: geometry, positions, UVs when contiguous, the byte runs, with an
   SSE2 XOR). It codes mesh_f32 and mm_f32 per unit once and transposes only the raw words between
   units, where it used to transpose the whole block and then overwrite. History lookups use a
   cursor (`Finder`, `Cursor`): a transaction lists its units in id order, so a lookup walks a
   node or two instead of a root-to-leaf path. The result is exactly `map.find`.
4. **Live buffers.** Each connection's /13 output buffer and typed meta are recycled once nothing
   else holds them (`FormState::spare_out`, `spare_meta`): no allocation, no zero fill. Every
   reuse that follows a `use_count() == 1` check takes an acquire fence, as HC5's copy-on-write
   sites do.

owner (`artifacts/render-stream/p3/w13f/harness/w13f-leg-cmd-cost.sh`):

- live only, `GRC_LIVE_MAX_CONNECTIONS=2`;
- one GSW viewer alternating /13 and /12 windows (join, 15 s settle, 30 s measure, 9 s

## Why the predictor stays at ~0.6 M cycles in the game

A scratchpad bench (`artifacts/render-stream/p3/w13f/bench/`) runs `rs13::Predictor` over
`pm7-a12-1`'s 4,954 patches in two patterns:

- the file lane's: one predictor;
- the live hub's: a `before` copy, a fresh output buffer, a memo entry holding `before` and
  `after`, released at the batch end, and the output recycled as `FormState` does.

It reports per-patch cycles and instructions, single pass. "cold" streams 64 MB between records,
as a game frame would.

| per patch, p50 | main, file | main, live | **W13f, live** |
| --- | --: | --: | --: |
| M instructions | 0.857 | 1.265 | **0.551** |
| M cycles, warm | 0.31 | 0.45 | **0.20** |
| M cycles, cold | 0.69–0.71 | 0.88–0.99 | **0.66–0.89** |
| release at the batch end, M cycles | — | 0.045 | 0.011 (the bench's floor) |

- **Instructions and warm cycles halve.** Cold cycles barely move, and the game sits at the cold
  end: 0.53–0.75 M cycles there, against W13z's 0.66–0.84.
- **The cost is in the history lookups.** The game's perf samples under `rs13::` on the encode
  thread (c3-1 combat B) split as:
  - 25 % on the map lookups (`body_refs`, `mm_refs`, `aabb_refs`, `same_shape`);
  - 18 % on the history copy (`next_state`, `give_bytes`, the map node copy);
  - 15 % in `code_words`, 23 % in `encode_typed`'s inline coding and copies, 6 % in
    `predict_meta`.
- **Why they are cold.** Each unit's H1 and H2 bytes live in their own heap buffer, behind map
  nodes, so every unit starts with a few cache misses (~135 units in a combat patch).

**The next lever** (not built): hold the history as references into the previous /12 records'
blocks instead of per-unit copies. A transaction lists its units in the order of the one before,
so H1 and H2 would then be two sequential streams beside the input. That removes the per-unit
copy into R(T), the pool and the map nodes on the hot path, at the cost of keeping each referenced
record alive while any of its units is uncarried. The wire-side lever is W13z's: a compact
residual that shrinks zstd's input. It belongs with the binary-syntax decision.

## Byte identity

- **The pm7-a12-1 bench output.** The /13 records of the file and live patterns, warm and cold,
  are one sha256 (`3dfe4285…`) for main and for every W13f build. W13p established that main's
  typed coding equals `rs-core-check transcode --to 13` on all 4,955 transactions.
- **`cmp-rec.ts` on capture-scale.** The full size, 600 frames, file mode, `bench-capture-scale.sh`.
  Main (`aab5e579`, built from `git archive`) against W13f is identical at /12 and at /13: 602 of
  602 records each, with only the session ids and the end timing stats allowed.
- **ctest 46/46.** This covers `rs13_codec` (golden-13 both ways, the invalid vectors, the
  sabotages) and `rs_publish`. It also covers `rs_live` and `rs_live_thread`: W13b's `w13-equiv`,
  sharing, order and resync tests, and HC5's `reorder` sabotage, all under `set_verify_meta`.

## New checks (`capture/test/rs_live_test.cpp`)

- **A lone /13 connection** allocates exactly 3 history states over 40 transactions. Beside two
  lockstep viewers, a /12 viewer, a resync and a late join, it reuses at least 3/4.
- **On the encode thread** (`Formation::Thread`), no prediction state is released on the main
  thread during the frame callbacks, and every /13 connection gets the same bytes as inline.
- **Mutations.** Reverting to W13p's behaviour (no spare, no batch-end reset) fails all three
  checks: 0 reused / 40 made, and 38 states released on the main thread. Disabling the spare alone
  fails the two reuse checks. Removing only the batch-end reset is caught by none, because the
  spare then holds each state's last reference. The reset stays as defence in depth, and it also
  moves the /13 bytes and metas off the main thread.

## Game runs

There were two launches (`w13f-c3-1`, `w13f-c3-2`), each under:

- the couch-coop lease (`exclusive:game:p3-sts2`, `shared:install`, own ports) before GSW's quiet
  lock (exclusive), with the load gate (< 4) outside both;
- a private farm and XDG (copied from W13z's, with the same mod) and `gamescope --backend headless`;
- `GRC_HOOK_LOG=off`, seats `--force-steam off`, nothing written under `~/.steam`, and nothing
  deployed into the install.

Both ended `survivors: none`, with all five hash sets unchanged (Steam remote, operator profile,
install binaries, install mods, mod build). The second launch exists because the code changed
after the first: items 3 (finders, XOR) and 4 came after `w13f-c3-1`.
